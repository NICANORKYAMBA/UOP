## Why Professional Email Matters

Email is one of the most common forms of formal communication in academic and professional settings. A well-crafted email demonstrates respect for the recipient's time, communicates ideas clearly, and increases the likelihood of a positive response (Ashman et al., 2021).

---

## Structure of a Professional Email

### 1. Subject Line
- Clear, specific, and informative
- Should summarize the purpose in 6–10 words
- Example: "Research Collaboration Inquiry – Cybersecurity in SMEs"

### 2. Salutation
- Use formal greeting: "Dear Dr. [Last Name]," or "Dear Professor [Last Name],"
- Avoid "Hey," "Hi there," or first names unless invited

### 3. Opening Paragraph
- State your purpose immediately
- Introduce yourself briefly (name, institution, program)
- Establish credibility or connection

### 4. Body Paragraph(s)
- Explain your research idea clearly and concisely
- Identify your research methodology and justify it
- State specifically what you are asking for (feedback, guidance, collaboration)
- Use persuasive but respectful language

### 5. Closing Paragraph
- Express appreciation for their time
- State your availability or next steps
- Invite a response

### 6. Sign-off
- Use formal closing: "Sincerely," "Best regards," "Respectfully,"
- Include full name, institution, program, and contact information

## Persuasive Writing in Emails

According to Weaver et al. (2016), persuasive writing aims to convince the reader to adopt a viewpoint or take an action by presenting logical arguments, credible evidence, and an appropriate emotional appeal (Section 3.8). In professional emails, persuasion is achieved through:

- **Logos** (logic): Clear reasoning for why the research matters
- **Ethos** (credibility): Demonstrating your qualifications and seriousness
- **Pathos** (emotion): Showing genuine enthusiasm and respect

Last (n.d.) emphasizes that persuasive writing must anticipate the reader's concerns and address them proactively. In a research email, this means acknowledging the advisor's expertise and explaining how the collaboration benefits both parties.

---

## Tone and Clarity

- **Formal but not stiff**: Professional tone without being overly bureaucratic
- **Concise**: Respect the reader's time — get to the point quickly
- **Active voice**: "I am conducting research on..." not "Research is being conducted..."
- **Specific**: Avoid vague statements like "I want to do research" — be precise about your topic and goals
- **Proofread**: Spelling and grammar errors undermine credibility

---

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

| Mistake | Better Approach |
|---------|----------------|
| Vague subject line | Be specific: include topic and purpose |
| No self-introduction | Always state who you are and your affiliation |
| Asking too much at once | Focus on one clear request |
| Overly long email | Keep to 200–350 words |
| Informal language | Maintain formal register throughout |
| No call to action | End with a clear, specific request |

## Email Template for Research Inquiry

```
Subject: Research Inquiry – [Topic] – [Your Name]

Dear Dr. [Last Name],

My name is [Full Name], a [year] student in [Program] at [Institution]. 
I am writing to seek your guidance on a research project I am developing 
on [brief topic description].

[Body: Explain research idea, methodology, and specific request]

I would greatly appreciate any feedback or guidance you could offer. 
I am available at your convenience and can be reached at [email/phone].

Thank you for your time and consideration.

Sincerely,
[Full Name]
[Program, Institution]
[Email] | [Phone]
```
