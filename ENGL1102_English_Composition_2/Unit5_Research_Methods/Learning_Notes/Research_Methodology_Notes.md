## What Are Research Methods?

Research methods are systematic procedures used to collect, analyze, and interpret data to answer research questions. Choosing the right method depends on the nature of the research question, the type of data needed, and the goals of the study (Serdikoff, 2021, p. 36).

---

## 1. Qualitative Research

**Definition**: Explores complex human experiences, meanings, and social phenomena through non-numerical data such as interviews, observations, and text analysis.

**Key Characteristics**:
- Seeks to understand *why* and *how*
- Data: words, themes, narratives, observations
- Small, purposive samples
- Flexible, emergent design
- Researcher is the primary instrument

**Common Methods**:
- Interviews (structured, semi-structured, unstructured)
- Focus groups
- Ethnography
- Case studies
- Content/thematic analysis

**When to Use**:
- Exploring new or poorly understood phenomena
- Understanding lived experiences
- Generating hypotheses for future quantitative research

**Example Research Question**: "How do first-generation college students experience academic advising?"

---

## 2. Quantitative Research

**Definition**: Examines relationships between variables using numerical data and statistical analysis to test hypotheses and generalize findings.

**Key Characteristics**:
- Seeks to measure *what*, *how much*, and *how often*
- Data: numbers, statistics, measurements
- Large, random samples for generalizability
- Structured, predetermined design
- Objective, controlled conditions

**Common Methods**:
- Surveys and questionnaires
- Experiments
- Correlational studies
- Longitudinal studies

**Quantitative Research Questions** (National University, 2024):
- Descriptive: "What percentage of students use cybersecurity tools?"
- Comparative: "Is there a difference in cybersecurity awareness between SMEs and large firms?"
- Relationship: "What is the relationship between employee training frequency and security breach rates?"

**Sampling Methods** (National University, 2024):
| Method | Description |
|--------|-------------|
| Random sampling | Every member has equal chance of selection |
| Stratified sampling | Population divided into subgroups, random sample from each |
| Convenience sampling | Readily available participants |
| Purposive sampling | Deliberately selected based on specific criteria |

**When to Use**:
- Testing hypotheses
- Measuring the extent of a phenomenon
- Generalizing findings to a larger population

**Example Research Question**: "What is the correlation between cybersecurity training frequency and the number of security incidents in small businesses?"

---

## 3. Mixed-Methods Research

**Definition**: Combines qualitative and quantitative approaches in a single study to provide a more complete understanding of the research problem.

**Key Characteristics**:
- Integrates both numerical and narrative data
- Compensates for weaknesses of each individual approach
- More comprehensive and nuanced findings
- Requires more time and resources

**Common Designs**:
- **Sequential Explanatory**: Quantitative first, then qualitative to explain results
- **Sequential Exploratory**: Qualitative first, then quantitative to test findings
- **Concurrent Triangulation**: Both collected simultaneously, compared

**When to Use**:
- When one method alone cannot fully answer the research question
- When you need both breadth (quantitative) and depth (qualitative)
- When validating findings across methods

**Example Research Question**: "How prevalent is cybersecurity non-compliance among SMEs (quantitative), and what organizational factors explain this behavior (qualitative)?"

---

## Choosing the Right Methodology

| Factor | Qualitative | Quantitative | Mixed-Methods |
|--------|-------------|--------------|---------------|
| Research goal | Explore/understand | Measure/test | Both |
| Data type | Words/themes | Numbers/stats | Both |
| Sample size | Small | Large | Both |
| Flexibility | High | Low | Moderate |
| Generalizability | Low | High | Moderate-High |
| Time required | Moderate | Moderate | High |

**Key Question**: Does your research question ask *what/how much* (quantitative) or *why/how* (qualitative)?
