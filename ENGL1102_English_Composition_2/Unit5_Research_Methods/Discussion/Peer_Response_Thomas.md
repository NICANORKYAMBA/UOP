## Response to Thomas Bisaccio

Hi Thomas,

Your email is well-crafted and your research hypothesis is genuinely interesting — the distinction between pathological smartphone use and intentional academic use is a meaningful gap in the literature, and framing it around a threshold model adds real analytical precision to your study.

Your justification for mixed-methods is strong, but I want to offer a methodological refinement worth considering. You describe using structural equation modelling (SEM) to identify the addiction threshold quantitatively, and structured interviews to define intentionality qualitatively. However, structured interviews — which follow a fixed script — may actually limit your ability to explore how students conceptualize intentionality, since that concept is likely to vary significantly across individuals. Semi-structured interviews, which provide guiding questions while allowing participants to elaborate freely, would give you richer data on this dimension. As Serdikoff (2021) explains, semi-structured interviews are specifically designed to balance consistency with the flexibility needed for deeper qualitative insight (Section 36). This adjustment would strengthen the qualitative component without compromising comparability across participants.

I also want to commend your use of the Li et al. (2024) SEM study as a methodological model. Grounding your quantitative design in an established analytical framework from a peer-reviewed source is exactly the kind of evidence-based justification that Ashman et al. (2021) describe as essential for persuasive professional communication — it demonstrates to your advisor that your methodology is not only theoretically sound but also practically feasible.

Your email is persuasive, well-organized, and impressively well-sourced. Well done!
