## Response to Kgotatso Nyadzane

Hi Kgotatso,

Your email is exceptionally well-written — the geographic specificity of focusing on urban South Africa, combined with the temporal framing of "during and after COVID-19," gives your study a clearly defined scope that strengthens both its feasibility and its contribution to the literature.

Your mention of the pragmatic paradigm as the philosophical foundation for your mixed-methods design is a sophisticated touch that most students overlook. It signals to your advisor that you understand not just the mechanics of mixed-methods but the epistemological reasoning behind choosing it. I want to build on this by suggesting a specific design that would align well with your stated goals. Since you aim to both identify prevalence patterns and explain why those trends occur, a **sequential explanatory design** would be particularly appropriate — administer the quantitative surveys first to identify which student subgroups report the highest anxiety and lowest motivation, then use those findings to purposively select participants for your semi-structured interviews. This ensures your qualitative phase directly interrogates the patterns your quantitative data reveals, rather than running in parallel without integration. Serdikoff (2021) emphasizes that the strength of mixed-methods lies precisely in this kind of deliberate integration between phases, rather than simply collecting both types of data independently (Section 36).

One additional consideration: since your study involves mental health data from students, you will want to address ethical safeguards in your methodology section — particularly informed consent and participant confidentiality — as these are essential for gaining institutional approval and maintaining research integrity (National University, 2024).

Your email is clear, persuasive, and professionally structured. Well done!
