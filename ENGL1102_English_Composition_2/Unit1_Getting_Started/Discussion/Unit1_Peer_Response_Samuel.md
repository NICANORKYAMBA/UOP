## Peer Response

Hi Samuel,

Your discussion on social media's impact on university students' academic performance is both timely and highly relevant to our generation. I appreciate how systematically you applied the pre-writing strategies and scientific method to develop your research question. Your hypothesis—that students spending more than three hours daily on social media will demonstrate lower academic performance—is specific, measurable, and testable, which are essential qualities for scientific inquiry (Serdikoff, 2021).

I found your brainstorming approach particularly effective. By identifying related concepts like study habits, time management, and multitasking, you've recognized that social media's impact isn't isolated but interconnected with broader behavioral patterns. This holistic view strengthens your research foundation. Your mention of controlling variables such as course load and study time demonstrates methodological rigor that would enhance the validity of any future study (Creswell & Creswell, 2018).

However, I'd like to offer a perspective that might add nuance to your hypothesis. While your research question focuses on the negative effects of social media, you briefly mentioned that social media "can also support academic collaboration." This is an important consideration that deserves deeper exploration. Recent research suggests that social media platforms like WhatsApp, Discord, and LinkedIn can facilitate peer learning, resource sharing, and academic networking (Greenhow & Lewin, 2016). Students often use these platforms to form study groups, share lecture notes, and clarify course concepts outside classroom hours.

**Here's a question for further consideration**: Could the relationship between social media use and academic performance be more complex than a simple negative correlation? Perhaps the *type* of social media use (educational vs. entertainment) and the *timing* of use (during study sessions vs. during breaks) matter more than total hours spent online. Your methodology could be strengthened by distinguishing between productive academic use and purely recreational scrolling.

Additionally, your three-hour threshold is interesting. What informed this specific cutoff? Research by Karpinski et al. (2013) found that even moderate social media use can impact GPA, but the relationship isn't always linear. Some students might effectively integrate social media into their learning strategies, while others become distracted at lower usage levels. Exploring individual differences in self-regulation and digital literacy could provide valuable insights.

Your application of the scientific method demonstrates how pre-writing strategies evolve into rigorous research frameworks. The progression from observation to hypothesis to data collection methodology shows clear academic thinking. As you move forward with this topic, consider whether your research might reveal not just *if* social media affects performance, but *how* and *under what conditions* it does so.

Thank you for sharing such a well-structured discussion. Your topic has significant implications for how universities might design interventions to help students balance digital engagement with academic success. I look forward to seeing how your research develops!
