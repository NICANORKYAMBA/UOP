Excellent post! Your explanation of network fundamentals is thorough and well-structured. I particularly appreciate how you distinguished between hubs, switches, and routers with clear functional explanations that business owners can understand.

Regarding your discussion question about connecting two LANs across town, a VPN (Virtual Private Network) router would be the most critical device for securely connecting the two separate locations into a single private network (Chauhan & Jangra, 2020). VPN routers create an encrypted tunnel over the public internet, allowing the two LANs to communicate as if they were on the same private network. This is more cost-effective than leasing dedicated private lines while maintaining security.

Two key security considerations when implementing this connection are encryption and authentication. First, implement strong encryption protocols like IPsec or SSL/TLS to protect data transmitted between locations from interception or eavesdropping (Borodin, 2024). Without encryption, sensitive business data traveling over public internet infrastructure remains vulnerable to man-in-the-middle attacks. Second, establish robust authentication mechanisms including multi-factor authentication for VPN access and strong pre-shared keys or digital certificates. This prevents unauthorized devices or users from connecting to the private network, ensuring only legitimate employees at both locations can access shared resources.

Your recommendation of star topology with full-duplex communication is spot-on for scalability. As the business grows to multiple locations, this foundation makes VPN implementation straightforward.
