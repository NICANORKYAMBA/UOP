Outstanding post! Your systematic breakdown of network fundamentals demonstrates strong understanding of networking concepts. I particularly appreciate your clear comparison of topologies and their practical implications for small businesses.

Regarding your discussion question about topology impact on scalability, star topology significantly enhances a small business's ability to scale operations as employees and devices increase. Star topology offers modular expansion—adding new devices simply requires connecting them to available switch ports or adding additional switches to the central infrastructure (Chauhan & Jangra, 2020). This plug-and-play scalability minimizes network downtime during expansion. When a business grows from 10 to 50 employees, administrators can add switches incrementally without redesigning the entire network architecture.

Star topology also provides centralized management, making it easier to monitor network performance, troubleshoot issues, and implement security policies as the network grows (Borodin, 2024). Network administrators can manage all connections from the central switch location, simplifying tasks like bandwidth allocation, access control, and traffic monitoring. In contrast, bus topology becomes increasingly problematic with scale—adding devices degrades performance for all users since they share a single communication line. Mesh topology, while highly reliable, becomes prohibitively expensive as device count increases because connection requirements grow exponentially.

Additionally, star topology supports network segmentation through VLANs (Virtual LANs), allowing businesses to logically separate departments or device types as they grow, improving both security and performance without physical infrastructure changes.

Your emphasis on full-duplex transmission complements star topology perfectly, as modern switches support full-duplex on all ports simultaneously, ensuring performance scales linearly with device additions.
