## (a) Seismograph Readings for 7.7 and 5.7

Rewriting M = log₁₀(x / 0.001) in exponential form gives x = 0.001 × 10^M (Abramson, 2023).

- M = 7.7: x = 0.001 × 10^7.7 = 10^4.7 ≈ **50,118.72 mm**
- M = 5.7: x = 0.001 × 10^5.7 = 10^2.7 ≈ **501.19 mm**

Comparing them: 50,118.72 ÷ 501.19 = 10² = **100**. Just 2 points on the Richter scale means the
7.7 earthquake's waves are 100 times larger, so it is far more powerful.

## (b) Why a Logarithmic Function?

Seismograph readings range from fractions of a millimetre to tens of metres. A logarithm
compresses that range into small numbers, and each whole step means a tenfold change,
just like the pH scale (Mathispower4u, 2011). A polynomial cannot do this, because it still
grows without limit for large readings and has no fixed "times ten" meaning per step. An
exponential function does the opposite, stretching large readings into even larger numbers. As the
exponential's inverse, the logarithm turns multiplying the amplitude into adding to the magnitude.

## (c) An Earthquake Felt in Kenya

On 7 May 2025, a magnitude 4.5 earthquake struck 81 km west-northwest of Longido, Tanzania, and
was felt in Nairobi and Machakos (Kirambia, 2025). Its reading would be x = 0.001 × 10^4.5 =
10^1.5 ≈ **31.62 mm**. That is about 1,585 times smaller than the 7.7 event, which fits the weak,
brief shaking that residents described (Muthama, 2025).
